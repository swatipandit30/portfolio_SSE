Six years of making systems talk to each other

I’m a Senior Software Engineer with 6 years of experience in cloud, integration and DevOps. I’ve led development teams across enterprise integration, data migration and cloud automation, and I do my best work owning business-critical systems from investigation and design through release and support.

I work across AWS and Azure, build event-driven integrations with Salesforce and Bullhorn, and use AI assistants every day to move faster without lowering quality.

I started in 2020 as a fresh graduate at TCS and was promoted twice in three years, first from trainee to System Engineer and then to TCS Digital. I worked on two major projects there, for a leading US cards and gifts company and for one of India’s biggest electronics retailers, learning how cloud, DevOps and full-stack code fit together.

Today I design and connect the integrations behind ’s recruiting platform and lead the engineers who run it. What I enjoy most is the hard, unglamorous middle: untangling an old system, moving it without anyone losing a day of work, and leaving behind clean code, clear documentation and people who can keep it running.

I mentor because good systems should outlast their authors, and along the way I’ve taught, spoken about AI and joined hackathons and meetups. Away from the keyboard you’ll usually find me reading, travelling or hunting for a new place to eat.

How I lead

Three habits that guide me, and six real situations behind them. Flip a card to see what happened.

Scope, plan, release

I break a large change into work the team can own, delegate it, and set the release standard before anything ships.

Led the development team on a 6-month migration and delivered 94% of planned work.

Grow the team

When the only senior expert left, I took over the platform, learned all of it, and then coached junior engineers until they could run it.

Turned a one-developer dependency into a trained team, and did the same kind of mentoring at TCS.

Align across teams

Partner teams, business teams and engineering want different things. I keep them aligned on one plan and one definition of done.

Resolved a critical partner-team escalation through multi-team collaboration.

Lead by owning the hard parts

I take the problem nobody wants, learn it all the way down, and then make sure someone else can carry it next time.
